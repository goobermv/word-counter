The Quick Brown Fox and the Go Programming Language.

The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. This classic pangram contains every letter of the English alphabet at least once. It is often used to test typewriters, keyboards, fonts, and now, our Go word counter. The quick brown fox is fast, but is it faster than a goroutine? The lazy dog sleeps, much like a program waiting on a channel.

Go, also known as Golang, is a statically typed, compiled programming language designed at Google by Robert Griesemer, Rob Pike, and Ken Thompson. It is syntactically similar to C, but with memory safety, garbage collection, structural typing, and CSP-style concurrency. The language was announced in November 2009 and is used in some of Google's production systems, as well as by many other businesses. 

Concurrency is a core part of Go. Goroutines are lightweight threads managed by the Go runtime. You can spawn thousands, even millions of them. Channels are the pipes that connect concurrent goroutines, allowing them to communicate with one another and synchronize their execution. Don't communicate by sharing memory; instead, share memory by communicating. This is a fundamental Go proverb.
